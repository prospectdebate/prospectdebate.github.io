The Program

Twenty minutes
a round.

Parliamentary debate is the fastest, most transferable thing you can do in high school — and Prospect is rebuilding a program that used to do it at a national level. Here's what it is, and what it gives you.

The Rebuild

This program has stood on national championship stages. It has out-argued schools ten times its size. Then it went quiet. We are bringing it back — and you can be here for the part people talk about.

What You Gain

Twenty minutes buys
more than you think.

Debate is the rare activity that pays out everywhere else — every class, every interview, every argument you'll ever win politely.

01

Think Under Pressure

Twenty minutes to build a case from nothing, on a topic you just learned. It rewires how fast you organize your thoughts — permanently.

02

Speak So People Listen

Standing and holding a room is a trained skill, not a personality trait. You will be pushed to build it until it looks effortless.

03

A Real Partnership

Parli is two-person. You prep together, win together, lose together. It's the closest thing school offers to a real team of two.

04

Research That Transfers

Current events, economics, philosophy, policy — you build a working knowledge of the world that shows up in essays and interviews.

05

The Circuit

Stanford. Berkeley. Tournaments across California and online, against schools that don't know your name yet. They will.

06

Ground-Floor Ownership

This is a rebuild. Early members don't inherit a program — they shape one. When it rises, it has your fingerprints on it.

The Format

How a round works.

Parliamentary debate is the fastest format there is: a new motion every round, and everything you say is built in the prep room minutes before.

T–20:00

The motion drops

A topic is announced — policy, values, current events. You've never seen it before. Neither has the other team.

T–19:59

Prep starts

Twenty minutes with your partner to define the debate, build your case, and predict everything the other side will say.

T–00:00

You walk in

Forty minutes of speeches. No notes handed to you, no scripts. The judge votes on who argued better — that's the whole game.
